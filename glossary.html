THE CYBER GLOSSARY

Shared language.
Sharper conversations.

Plain-language explanations of terms used across this site. A practical starting point, without the acronym overload.

26 terms shown

Operations

Alert triage

The first review of an alert to establish its meaning, priority, and next investigation step.

In practice

A starting assessment should identify supporting evidence and unresolved questions.

Risk

Asset inventory

A record of the systems, software, identities, and services an organization operates.

In practice

An advisory becomes actionable when it can be connected to an actual asset and owner.

Risk

Attack surface

The exposed paths through which someone could interact with or attempt to compromise a system.

In practice

Reachable services, identities, software interfaces, and trust relationships all matter.

Identity

Authentication

The process of verifying a claimed identity.

In practice

Successful authentication does not automatically establish that the activity was legitimate.

Identity

Authorization

The decision about what an authenticated identity is permitted to do.

In practice

Authentication and permission are separate questions.

Detection

Baseline

A reference pattern used to compare current behavior with expected activity.

In practice

A changed pattern is a lead to investigate, not automatically a malicious event.

Operations

Containment

Response measures intended to limit further harm or spread during an incident.

In practice

Choose and verify measures through the authorized response process.

Vulnerabilities

CVE

A Common Vulnerabilities and Exposures identifier for a publicly disclosed vulnerability.

In practice

An identifier helps connect advisories and references; it does not determine your organization’s risk by itself.

Vulnerabilities

CVSS

The Common Vulnerability Scoring System describes technical characteristics and severity of vulnerabilities.

In practice

A score is one input to prioritization, alongside exposure, exploitation, and business context.

Detection

Detection engineering

The practice of designing, testing, maintaining, and improving security detections.

In practice

Good detection logic depends on reliable source data and meaningful validation.

Operations

EDR

Endpoint Detection and Response tools collect endpoint activity and support investigation and response.

In practice

Tool presence alone does not prove that a relevant event was observed or blocked.

Detection

False positive

A result treated as a detection match even though the behavior of interest did not occur.

In practice

Document the reason for the classification before changing a rule.

Intelligence

Indicator of compromise

An observable artifact, such as a domain or file hash, that may be associated with malicious activity.

In practice

Indicator reputation should be interpreted alongside telemetry and context.

Vulnerabilities

KEV

CISA’s Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog identifies vulnerabilities with evidence of exploitation in the wild.

In practice

Catalog inclusion supports prioritization; absence from the catalog does not establish safety.

Identity

Least privilege

Granting only the permissions needed for a particular responsibility.

In practice

Apply it to people, services, and AI tools, and review access as responsibilities change.

Identity

MFA

Multi-factor authentication uses more than one type of authentication factor.

In practice

Its effectiveness depends on the method, configuration, enrollment, and recovery paths.

Operations

Observability

The ability to understand system behavior through available outputs such as logs, metrics, and traces.

In practice

Missing or stale telemetry limits what an investigation can conclude.

Operations

Playbook

A documented process for handling a particular operational or security scenario.

In practice

A playbook should identify decision points, responsibilities, and verification steps.

AI security

Prompt injection

Untrusted content attempts to influence an AI system’s behavior or redirect its task.

In practice

Treat retrieved material as data, and keep tool permissions and sensitive actions under independent controls.

Intelligence

Provenance

Information about where an artifact, claim, or piece of evidence came from.

In practice

Source and collection context help an analyst assess reliability.

Risk

Risk

The potential for harm considered in the context of likelihood, consequences, and relevant uncertainty.

In practice

A useful assessment states assumptions and what would change the decision.

Operations

SIEM

Security Information and Event Management combines security data to support detection, investigation, and reporting.

In practice

Useful results depend on source coverage, data quality, and detection design.

Operations

SOAR

Security Orchestration, Automation, and Response connects tools and workflows for security operations.

In practice

Automation should have defined permissions, approval boundaries, and failure handling.

Detection

Telemetry

Recorded activity and measurements produced by systems and security tools.

In practice

Check event freshness, completeness, and field quality before relying on an absence of results.

Detection

Threat hunting

A structured investigation that proactively tests hypotheses about possible adversary behavior.

In practice

A hunt can produce validated findings, rejected hypotheses, or a visible coverage gap.

Intelligence

TTPs

Tactics, techniques, and procedures describe adversary goals, methods, and ways of carrying them out.

In practice

Behavioral context can remain useful even when individual indicators change.
