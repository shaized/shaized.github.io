FIELDNOTE 01 / DETECTION ENGINEERING

A quiet detection
needs an explanation.

No alerts can mean no activity. It can also mean missing logs, a schema change, or a rule that no longer matches reality.

Open fieldnote +

Start with three separate checks: is the source arriving, can the query match expected events, and does the scope include the assets you intend to protect?

Then review a known positive and a benign example. Record what the detection is designed to observe, the evidence it needs, and who owns it. An enabled rule is a configuration state; validated coverage is an engineering result.

Reference: MITRE ATT&CK ↗
FIELDNOTE 02 / AI SECURITY

Give agents a job.
Give them a boundary.

The quality of an AI workflow depends on more than its answer. Tool permissions, data access, and review paths shape the risk.

Open fieldnote +

Separate observation from action. Give each capability only the tools and data needed for its job, treat retrieved content as untrusted, and validate structured outputs before downstream use.

A useful evaluation includes incomplete evidence, malicious instructions inside source material, and disagreement with the analyst. Preserve uncertainty and require explicit authorization for consequential actions.

Reference: OWASP Gen AI risks ↗
FIELDNOTE 03 / THREAT PRIORITIZATION

Turn “critical”
into a decision.

A vendor’s severity describes a vulnerability. Your response also depends on where it runs, whether it is reachable, and what an attacker could gain.

Open fieldnote +

Combine exploitation evidence, asset exposure, business impact, and verified compensating controls. Distinguish “not observed” from “not affected,” and state what evidence would change the assessment.

The result should identify an owner, an action, and a review trigger. Record assumptions so a new exploit report or configuration change can lead to a deliberate reassessment.

Reference: CISA exploited-vulnerability catalog ↗
